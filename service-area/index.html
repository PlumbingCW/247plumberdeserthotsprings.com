Desert Hot Springs

Our home market is the whole city, including ZIP codes 92240 and 92241. That covers the Palm Drive and Pierson Boulevard core, the original townsite, the spa district known for its hot mineral water, and the newer subdivisions toward Dillon Road. Call and the dispatcher will confirm your address.

The northern edge of the city is moving from septic to sewer. Mission Springs Water District runs the M2 Septic to Sewer Project in that area, between San Gorgonio Street, Dillon Road, Yerxa Road and Avenida Gracia. If you live there, tell us whether you are on septic or sewer when you call, and see sewer line cleaning for what changes.

How far we travel and how fast

We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic, the job before yours and the nature of your problem all change it. What we do promise is an honest answer. When you call, the dispatcher tells you the earliest appointment open for your address, and if that changes, you hear about it.

Distances here are short. Desert Hot Springs, Palm Springs, North Palm Springs and Thousand Palms are all connected by Interstate 10 and the roads that feed it, and Morongo Valley sits a little farther out on State Route 62. Tell us your address and cross street, and we will tell you what to expect.

Coverage notes for rentals and second homes

Many properties across the Coachella Valley are rentals or second homes, and a plumbing problem can start while nobody is there. If you manage a property from a distance, give the dispatcher the owner contact, the access instructions and a neighbor or manager who can meet the plumber. We confirm authorization before we start work, and we send a summary of what we found.

A short checklist helps. Know where the main water valve is, keep a spare key or lockbox code ready, and tell guests who to call. For a hidden leak at an empty property, a meter check is the fastest first step, and our leak detection page explains it.

Septic, sewer and what it means for service

Plumbers in this area need to know whether a house uses a sewer main or a septic tank, because the same symptom can mean different things. A slow main drain on sewer is a blocked line. On septic it can be a full tank or a failing drain field. Our dispatcher asks, and so should any plumber you call.

If you are not sure, check your bill from the water district or ask a neighbor. Mission Springs Water District can confirm sewer service for a Desert Hot Springs address at 760-329-6448. Knowing the answer before we arrive saves time, and it prevents the wrong fix.

What to know before you call

  • Give the dispatcher your full address and the nearest cross street
  • Say whether the property is on sewer or septic
  • Describe what is happening, such as water flowing or a smell
  • Tell us if you can reach the main water valve
  • Share the age of the home, since older homes often have older pipe
