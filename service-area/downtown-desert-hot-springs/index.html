Downtown is the original townsite and the part of the city with the longest history. It centers on the crossing of Palm Drive and Pierson Boulevard.

Where downtown sits

Desert Hot Springs was founded by L. W. Coffee on July 12, 1941, and the original townsite was only one square mile, centered on Palm Drive and Pierson Boulevard. That crossing is still the center of the downtown study area.

The City of Desert Hot Springs studied the area in a 2025 Downtown Existing Conditions Report. Its study area is a T-shape of about 173.5 acres, bounded by 8th Street on the north, Mesquite Avenue on the east, Hacienda Avenue on the south and West Drive on the west.

Homes and storefronts on the same blocks

The report describes streets around Palm Drive and Pierson Boulevard as dominated by low-density housing, with businesses such as restaurants, churches and repair shops on corners. It also notes vacant and underused storefronts, mostly along Pierson Boulevard and Palm Drive.

For plumbing, that mix matters. A house and a small shop on the same block share the same neighborhood but not the same needs. One has a kitchen and two bathrooms, and the other may have a restroom that customers use all day.

What we are not claiming

The City report does not give a downtown housing count, and it does not say what pipe material is under any street. Citywide, the City housing element says 52 percent of homes were built before 1990 and 20 percent before 1970. That is a city figure, so ask the date of your own building before assuming anything.

Plumbing for homes near Palm Drive

Slow or repeat drains

A drain that clears and returns points to something further down the line. Drain cleaning clears the blockage, and a camera shows why it keeps coming back.

Sewer line concerns

If several fixtures back up together, the problem is usually the main line. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe from the cleanout.

Hidden leaks

A water bill that climbs with no change in use deserves a check. Leak detection finds the source before walls or floors are opened.

Fixtures that waste water

A running toilet or a dripping faucet adds up. See toilet repair for what a fill valve or flapper fix involves.

Plumbing for businesses on Pierson and Palm

Shops, galleries and cafes rely on working restrooms and clean drains during opening hours. A problem that is a nuisance at home can close a counter. Commercial plumbing covers restroom fixtures, kitchen drains and water heaters for small businesses.

If you rent your space, check your lease before calling. Some repairs fall to the landlord, and a plumber can write up what was found so you have a record to share.

Before you call about a downtown property

  • Note whether the building is a house, a shop or a mix of both
  • Find the main water shutoff and the sewer cleanout, if you can reach them safely
  • Write down which fixtures are affected and when it started
  • Know the approximate year the building was built, or say that you do not
  • Tell us whether you own the property or rent it
