Older neighborhoods and the plumbing behind them

Palm Springs was incorporated on April 20, 1938, and its neighborhoods have very different ages. The city lists areas such as Old Las Palmas, Vista Las Palmas, the Movie Colony, Racquet Club Estates and Tahquitz River Estates among its named neighborhoods. Established areas like these often have original supply pipe and old drain lines, which is why we ask the age of the house before we quote any repair.

Older homes may carry galvanized supply pipe or cast iron drains, and both wear from the inside. A house with rusty water or repeated drain clogs may be asking for more than another patch. See repiping if the supply side is failing, and sewer camera inspection if the drains keep backing up.

Vacation rentals, second homes and short notice

Palm Springs has many second homes and rentals, and a leak in an empty house can run for days before anyone notices. If you manage a property from out of town, a meter check and a leak detection visit can catch a hidden problem early. We can meet a property manager or a neighbor at the door, as long as the owner authorizes the visit.

Rental turnover also wears out fixtures, water heaters and disposals. Our water heater repair and garbage disposal pages explain what we fix on a typical visit, and commercial plumbing covers small hotels and multi-unit buildings.

Sewer, water and who to call

The City of Palm Springs runs its own sewer system and wastewater treatment plant, so a main line problem past the property line is a city matter.

Water comes from a district rather than from the city itself. Check your water bill to see whether Desert Water Agency or another provider serves your address. The agency lists a phone number of (760) 323-4971 and publishes an annual water quality report, so we do not guess at hardness. Test your tap or read the report.

We repair the private side of your plumbing, which is everything from the meter or property line to the fixtures. If we find the problem is on the public side, we tell you and give you what we saw to share with the utility.

Permits and inspections in Palm Springs

The City of Palm Springs Building and Safety Department enforces the plumbing code and issues permits at 3200 E Tahquitz Canyon Way. Its public counter lists hours of 7:30 am to 5:30 pm Monday through Thursday. Water heater replacement and repiping are typically permitted work under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and we can explain what the department asks for before the job starts.

Desert summers are hard on plumbing. Palm Springs averages under five inches of rain a year, and long dry spells dry out outdoor fittings and hose bibs, while heat bakes exposed lines. If you see a cracked outdoor valve or a wet patch where no sprinkler runs, our water line repair page explains how we trace and fix a buried leak.
