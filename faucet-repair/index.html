A faucet that drips once per second can waste thousands of gallons a year, and the fix is often a single inexpensive part.

If the faucet is spraying or will not shut off, close the shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If there is no valve, shut off the main. For an active leak that cannot be stopped, call the 24/7 line. Otherwise, same-week visits are common.

Why faucets fail

Worn cartridge or washer

The most common cause of drips from the spout in single handle and compression faucets.

Corroded valve seat

Mineral and corrosion cut grooves where the seal should close.

Mineral scale on the aerator

White crust restricts flow and makes the spray uneven.

Leaks at the base or handle

O-rings wear out and let water seep around the stem.

Loose or cracked fittings

Supply lines and connections loosen over time.

Low pressure at one fixture

Often a clogged aerator or cartridge, not a pressure problem in the house.

Hard water and desert faucets

White crust on a faucet is mineral scale, and it builds up fastest where water evaporates. Mission Springs Water District publishes an annual water quality report, so we leave out a hardness figure and suggest you test your own tap. Soaking an aerator in vinegar clears light scale, and heavy buildup may need a new cartridge.

If scale is a constant fight, a water treatment system can help. Our water softener and filtration page explains the options and what a system does and does not fix.

Kitchen, bath and outdoor fixtures

Kitchen faucets take the most abuse because of pull-down sprayers and constant use. Bath faucets fail from scale and soap. Outdoor hose bibs in the desert crack from sun and age, and a hose bib that leaks inside the wall can damage the wall without a visible drip. We repair or replace all three, and we replace the shutoff valves under the sink if they will not close.

If you are updating a kitchen or bath, tell us the fixture model. A homeowner-bought faucet is fine to install. For new installs of sinks, tubs and shower valves, see plumbing installation.

Replacement faucets and supply lines should be certified for drinking water use, as the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects.

What a faucet repair visit looks like

We start by shutting off the supply and opening the faucet to see what failed. Many repairs take less than an hour. A cartridge or seat swap is the most common job, and we bring a range of cartridges so the first trip is often the last one.

If the faucet is an older model with parts that are no longer made, we say so and quote a replacement. A new faucet from a reputable maker is often cheaper than hunting for a discontinued part, and it comes with its own warranty from the manufacturer.

Shutoff valves under the sink

The small valves under a sink get ignored until the day they fail. A valve that will not turn or that drips at the stem can turn a simple faucet job into a flood. We test every valve on the visit and replace the ones that will not close fully. New braided supply lines are inexpensive and a good upgrade whenever a faucet is changed.

  • Turn each shutoff once a year so it does not seize
  • Replace rubber supply lines that are cracked, bulging or older than the faucet
  • Watch for a wet cabinet floor, which signals a slow leak
