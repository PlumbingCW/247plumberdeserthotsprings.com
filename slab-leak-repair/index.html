A slab leak can send hundreds of gallons into the ground under the house, which can undermine the slab, lift flooring and raise your bill. Our 24/7 line takes slab leak calls at night and on weekends. Turn off the water at the main, keep the area clear and, if the floor is wet, switch off the nearby circuit.

Signs of a slab leak

What homeowners notice first

  • A water meter that keeps moving with every fixture off
  • A warm or hot spot on a tile or concrete floor
  • The sound of water running when nothing is on
  • Wet carpet or lifting flooring with no obvious source
  • A sudden drop in hot water or water pressure
  • Cracks in walls or flooring that were not there before

Why slab leaks happen in the desert

Copper pipes in a slab sit in contact with concrete and soil for decades. Mineral content in the water, soil chemistry and vibration can wear a pinhole in a line. Slabs also shift. Desert Hot Springs sits along the Mission Creek Fault, a branch of the San Andreas, so lines under older slabs deal with movement as well as age.

That does not mean every slab leak is seismic. Most start as pinholes or joint failures. The point is practical: if your house is older, the same line may fail again, so we discuss the long-term fix as well as the immediate repair.

Insurance and documentation

Sudden water damage is often covered by a homeowner policy while slow wear may not be. We recommend photographing the area, keeping the leak detection findings and calling your insurer before repairs begin, so you know what paperwork they need. We can provide a written description of what we found.

If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager right away. A slab leak is typically the owner or manager to authorize, and we can speak with them directly about scope and cost.

Rerouted or replaced supply pipe has to meet the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and many slab repairs end with a pressure test.

What a slab leak costs you if you wait

The cost of waiting is more than a higher bill. Water under a slab can soften the soil, lift the floor and feed mold in the walls. Every week of delay adds to the repair. Many owners who have a leak found early save a great deal compared with those who wait until the floor buckles.

Ask your plumber for the leak location, the pipe condition and a written option list. Then ask your insurer what they need. Doing both before the repair saves headaches later.
