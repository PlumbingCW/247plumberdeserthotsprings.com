Finding the break before anything is dug

Guessing at a sewer problem is expensive. We run a camera through the line from the cleanout, watch the monitor and locate the break at the surface. The recording shows the damage, and it gives you a record to share with an insurer or a property manager.

Common findings include roots that entered a joint, a section that has bellied and holds water, a cracked or offset pipe, and old connections that do not match. Each one calls for a different repair, so the camera comes before the quote.

Septic and sewer: why Desert Hot Springs lines are not all alike

Part of northern Desert Hot Springs still runs on septic systems, and Mission Springs Water District is connecting about 695 properties to its sewer through the M2 Septic to Sewer Project. The project area runs from San Gorgonio Street on the north to Dillon Road on the south, and from Avenida Gracia on the west to Yerxa Road on the east.

If your property is inside that boundary, call the district at 760-329-6448 before you spend money on a private lateral replacement, because the connection work may be coordinated with the district schedule.

Permits and who to ask

The City of Desert Hot Springs Building Division states that replacing or relocating a water supply, sewer or drainage system is not ordinary exempt repair work. Clearing a stoppage and repairing a leak are listed as exempt, and the division also says emergency work can be permitted after the fact by applying within the next working business day. We recommend confirming the rule for your job with the division at (760) 329-6411.

If the job is a stoppage rather than a break, our drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning pages explain the difference.

Pipe size, slope and cleanout placement follow the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and an inspector checks them on permitted sewer work.
