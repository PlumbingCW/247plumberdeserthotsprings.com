Where to Install a Tankless Water Heater in a Desert Hot Springs, CA Home

The best place for a tankless water heater is a spot with a short vent path, room for a larger gas line, access for service and a drain nearby. For many Desert Hot Springs homes that means a garage wall, an exterior wall or a utility room. The right spot depends on your layout, and the wrong one can cost more than the unit.

A tankless unit hangs on a wall, so the question is which wall. Three things decide it: the vent, the gas line and the distance to your fixtures.

An exterior wall unit is rated for outdoor use and needs no vent through the house. It gets exposed to the elements, and desert sun is intense, so check that the unit is rated for it. A garage wall is the most common choice, since it is close to the old tank and has easy access.

Gas and vent needs at each spot

A tankless heater draws far more gas at once than a tank heater. The gas line to the unit often has to be larger, and the meter must be able to supply it. The farther the unit sits from the meter, the more the line size matters. A good plumber measures the run before recommending a spot.

The vent must lead outside, usually through a wall or the roof, with clearances to windows, doors and vents. Many units use a sealed vent in plastic or stainless pipe. Mixing parts between makers is not allowed. The maker instructions control the vent type and length.

Near the fixtures

  • Short hot water wait
  • Less pipe to insulate
  • May need a longer vent or gas run
  • May be a noisy room

Near the meter

  • Short, large gas line
  • Lower install cost
  • Longer hot water wait
  • May need a recirculation line

Desert Hot Springs: garages, heat and flushing access

Garages in Desert Hot Springs can get very hot in summer, and a closed garage can push a unit toward its temperature limits. Check the maker rating for ambient temperature, and keep the space ventilated.

Mission Springs Water District publishes an annual water quality report on its website, and mineral scale is the main maintenance issue for a tankless unit. Place the heater where you can reach the service valves to flush it, since a unit boxed behind shelves will never get serviced. Our tankless water heater page explains the flush schedule.

Clearances, vent materials and gas sizing for a tankless unit follow the maker instructions and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Clearances, drains and power

  • Leave clear space around the unit as the maker specifies
  • Keep stored items away from the vent and the front of the unit
  • Plan a condensate drain with a neutralizer for condensing models
  • Provide a nearby outlet for the controls and fan
  • Install isolation valves so the unit can be flushed
  • Add a pressure relief valve and a discharge line

These details seem small, but they are what an inspector checks. A unit that has no room around it overheats, and a unit with no outlet will not run. We walk the space with you and mark everything before we hang the unit.

Replacing a tank with tankless

Swapping a tank for tankless in the same spot is rarely a straight swap. The gas line, the vent and the water lines all change. A tank sits on the floor, and a tankless unit hangs on a wall, which may need blocking. If the budget is tight, a new tank heater is often the better value. Long pipe runs and the wait for hot water

A tankless unit does not shorten the wait for hot water at a faucet. The wait depends on how far the water has to travel, and a unit far from the bathroom still has a long cold run to clear. A recirculation line or a point of use unit near a distant bathroom can help.

Plan the pipe routes when you pick the spot. Insulating the hot line reduces heat loss, and a short run saves water. If the wait for hot water bothers you now, a tankless unit will not fix it, but a different location can.
