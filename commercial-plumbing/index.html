Commercial emergencies we handle

Main line backups

Multiple restrooms or floor drains backing up at once.

Grease line blockages

Burst or leaking supply pipes

Low pressure and valve problems

Restaurants and food service

Kitchens fill their lines with grease faster than any other business. A weekly pattern of slow drains usually means the line needs jetting, not another cable. We recommend hydro jetting on a schedule for busy kitchens, and we can help you document grease interceptor service for the health department.

Desert Hot Springs has a mix of local restaurants, spa and wellness businesses, and event venues that rely on hot water and clean drains. A failed heater or a backed up floor drain can force a closure, so scheduled checks pay for themselves.

Rentals, spas and multi-unit buildings

Desert Hot Springs is known for its hot mineral water, and many small hotels, spas and vacation rentals here run on heavy hot water use and constant guest turnover. Fixtures wear faster, and a leak in one unit can reach another. We work with owners, managers and HOAs, and we confirm who authorizes the work before starting.

Scheduled maintenance beats emergency calls

A preventive visit costs less than a Saturday night emergency. Typical items include jetting grease lines, checking water heaters and relief valves, testing shutoff valves, inspecting restrooms and confirming backflow devices are tested on time. We will give you a short written summary of what we find.

Permits apply to most commercial work beyond simple repair. The City of Desert Hot Springs Building Division handles them at (760) 329-6411, and we can help with the paperwork.

Commercial fixture counts, grease interceptors and backflow protection follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the local building department enforces it.

Restroom code, fixtures and customer-facing problems

Public restrooms fail in predictable ways. Flush valves stick, urinals leak at the supply, sink faucets loosen and floor drains smell when the trap dries out. A restroom that is out of order sends customers elsewhere, so we repair the fixture first and then look for what caused it.

Accessibility rules and fixture counts apply to many commercial spaces, and they change when a business remodels or changes use. We install and repair to the California Plumbing Code, and we tell you when a change needs a permit or an inspection before the work starts.

Hot water for kitchens, salons and spas

Businesses that depend on hot water cannot work without it. A restaurant needs it for the dish machine, a salon needs it at every shampoo bowl, and a spa needs it across dozens of guest rooms. Commercial heaters are sized differently from home units, and a failure often means a temporary fix first and a planned replacement second. Our water heater replacement page explains how we size and swap a unit.

Before we arrive at a business emergency

  • Shut off the main water valve or the valve for the affected area
  • Keep customers and staff out of wet or sewage-affected areas
  • Turn off power to equipment near standing water if it is safe to reach
  • Note which fixtures, drains and floors are affected
  • Photograph damage and keep receipts for your insurer
  • Have the owner or manager reachable to authorize work
  • Tell us the make and age of the heater so we bring the right parts
  • Note any recirculation pump or return line, since a failed pump looks like a failed heater
  • Say whether the building has a master shutoff and where it sits
