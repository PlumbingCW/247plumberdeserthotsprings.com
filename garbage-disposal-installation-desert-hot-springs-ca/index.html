Garbage Disposal Installation in Desert Hot Springs, CA: What to Know

A garbage disposal install takes about two hours when there is already a sink flange, a drain and a power source under the sink. The parts that slow it down are the wiring, the dishwasher connection and old drain pipe that does not line up. This guide walks through what the job involves.

Before you buy a disposal, check three things: an outlet or a switched wire under the sink, enough room below the drain, and whether your home is on sewer or septic.

What you need under the sink

A disposal bolts to the bottom of the sink drain, so it needs height. Most units are about a foot tall, and a deep sink or a tall bottle rack can leave very little room. Measure from the sink flange to the cabinet floor and subtract the trap arm. If the space is tight, a compact model fits better than a bigger motor.

The unit also needs power. Many kitchens have a switched outlet under the sink, and some have only a wall switch with no outlet. A plumber can plug the unit in if an outlet exists. If there is no power, an electrician needs to add it, and we tell you that before we quote the job.

How the install goes, step by step

1

Cut power and clear the cabinet

We switch off the breaker, empty the cabinet and place a bucket under the trap.

2

Remove the old unit or the old trap

The old disposal comes off the mounting ring, or the standard sink trap is removed for a first install.

3

Mount the new unit

We seat the flange with plumber putty, attach the mounting ring and hang the unit.

4

Connect and test

We reconnect the drain and dishwasher hose, plug in the unit and run water to check every joint.

Always test with water before you close the cabinet. A drip at the flange often shows up only after a minute of steady running, and a leak at the dishwasher hose appears when the dishwasher drains. We run both before we leave.

Septic or sewer decides whether a disposal is a good idea

A disposal sends ground food into the drain, and on a septic system that adds solids to the tank. A tank with a disposal fills faster and may need pumping more often. If you are on septic, think hard about whether you need one at all. Composting scraps is often easier on the tank.

On a sewer connection, a disposal is less of a worry for the system, but it still sends grease and fiber into your own pipes. Grease cools and sticks inside the drain, and a disposal does not stop that. Run cold water, avoid grease and keep fibrous scraps out. If a clog forms downstream, our drain cleaning page explains what we do.

Desert Hot Springs: septic, sewer and the M2 project

The project covers about 695 properties and roughly 405 aging septic systems, between San Gorgonio Street, Dillon Road, Yerxa Road and Avenida Gracia.

This matters for a disposal install. If your address is inside the project area, ask the district at 760-329-6448 about your connection schedule before you add a disposal that will load a septic tank you may soon abandon. Outside the project area, confirm what your property uses, since both septic and sewer homes exist in the city.

The drain connection for a disposal follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including the trap and the dishwasher air gap or high loop.

Mistakes we see most often

  • Leaving the dishwasher knockout plug in place, so the dishwasher cannot drain
  • Overtightening the mounting ring and cracking the flange
  • Skipping plumber putty or using silicone that never cures properly
  • Hardwiring a unit into a circuit that is too small
  • Forgetting to test the dishwasher hose before closing the cabinet
  • Putting grease, rice, pasta or fibrous scraps down the unit

Most of these mistakes appear a week or two after the install, when a slow leak damages the cabinet floor. A careful test on day one prevents that. Check under the sink with a flashlight after the first few uses.
