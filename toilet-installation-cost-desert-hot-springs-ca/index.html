What Affects Toilet Installation Cost in Desert Hot Springs, CA?

The cost of installing a toilet depends on five things: the toilet you choose, the condition of the flange and floor, the shutoff valve and supply line, the removal of the old toilet and the plumber labor. A simple swap on a sound flange costs far less than a job that needs a new flange or a floor repair. We do not post a price here because it depends on your bathroom.

The five things that set the price

The toilet itself can be a small part of the total. A plumber buying parts at a supply house can often match a store price. A homeowner who buys the toilet saves a little, but should check the rough-in distance first. That is the measure from the wall to the center of the drain, and the toilet must match it.

The flange and the floor

The flange is the ring on the floor that anchors the toilet and connects it to the drain. When it is cracked or sits too low, the new toilet will rock and leak. Repair kits exist for common flange problems. A rotted floor needs repair before a new toilet goes on, and that can double the job.

Slab homes are common here, so a floor that has been wet for a while may have damaged the tile, the grout or the layer below. Tell the plumber if the grout around the base is stained or the floor feels soft. We check the flange and the floor when we pull the old toilet.

Desert Hot Springs: water use and older toilets

Mission Springs Water District provides water and sewer service in the city, and its phone is 760-329-6448. Older toilets can use far more water per flush than current models, so a new efficient toilet lowers your bill over time.

If your home is in the northern part of the city, check whether you are on septic or sewer. Mission Springs is moving about 695 properties off septic through its M2 project. A more efficient toilet is easier on a septic tank, and our toilet repair page covers repair against replacement.

Toilet installation follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including clearances around the bowl and the connection to the drain.

How to compare quotes

  • Does the quote include the new wax ring, bolts and supply line?
  • Does it cover removal and haul away of the old toilet?
  • What happens if the flange or floor needs repair?
  • Is the shutoff valve included or extra?
  • Does the price assume you supply the toilet?

A low quote that leaves out the flange check can grow once the old toilet comes up. A clear quote lists what is included and what is not. We explain the price before the work starts and tell you about any change before we proceed.

Choosing a toilet that does not cause trouble

Check the rough-in distance, the bowl shape and the flush type. A strong gravity flush is simple and reliable, and some pressure assisted models are loud and need more parts. In a rental, a plain model with common parts means faster repairs. In a family bathroom, comfort height and elongated bowls are popular.

If you are redoing the bathroom, see plumbing installation for how fixture moves change the work. We serve Palm Springs and the rest of the service area, and the 24/7 line is open if a toilet overflows.

Extras that can add to the job

A few extras show up often. A new shutoff valve is common, since old valves tend to seize when turned. A new supply line is cheap insurance. A bidet attachment or an elongated seat adds a small amount of work. Haul away of the old toilet is usually included, but ask.

In a rental or a vacation home, a plumber may also check the water pressure, the tank water level and the other fixtures during the visit. A short checklist at turnover prevents the next emergency call. Ask what the plumber checks, and whether any extras are optional.
