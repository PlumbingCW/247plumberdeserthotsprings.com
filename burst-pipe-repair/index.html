What causes pipes to burst in the Coachella Valley

Desert Hot Springs sits at about 1,076 feet and gets less than six inches of rain a year, so we are not a deep-freeze market. Bursts here come from other causes. Pressure that runs too high, aging copper that pits through, a fitting that fails, a water heater connector that splits, and sun-baked outdoor lines and hose bibs that crack are the usual suspects.

Winter nights in the high desert can still dip close to freezing, so an exposed hose bib or a pipe in an uninsulated wall can freeze on a cold night. That is the exception rather than the rule, and we tell you plainly when the cause is pressure or age.

Pressure too high

A pressure reducing valve that has failed can send full street pressure into fixtures and pipe.

Corroded copper

Pinhole leaks and green stains show a pipe wearing through from the inside or the outside.

Failed connector

A flexible water heater or toilet supply line can split without warning.

Sun and wear on outdoor lines

Exposed hose bibs, irrigation fittings and plastic lines crack in desert sun.

What a burst pipe repair involves

We stop the leak at the source, cut out the damaged section and install new pipe and fittings that match the existing system. Copper gets soldered or pressed fittings, PEX gets crimp or expansion fittings, and PVC gets cemented joints. We then restore pressure, check every joint and look for the second weak spot, because a pipe that failed once often has a neighbor close behind.

If the pipe is inside a wall or ceiling, we locate the break and open the smallest section needed. If you suspect more than one leak, a leak detection visit finds the rest before walls are closed. If the pipe is the water service line outside the house, see water line repair.

Permits, rentals and who pays

The City of Desert Hot Springs Building Division lists repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures as exempt work, while replacing a concealed defective pipe is treated as new work needing a permit. It also says emergency work needs a permit application submitted within the next working business day. We confirm your case with the division at (760) 329-6411.

Renters should tell the landlord or property manager at once and take photos. Owners should call their insurer before big repairs begin.

Replacement pipe and fittings are chosen to match the system and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
