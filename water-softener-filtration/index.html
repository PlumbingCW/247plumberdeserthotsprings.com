The right system depends on what is in your water, so test first and buy second.

Mission Springs Water District provides drinking water to Desert Hot Springs and publishes an annual water quality report, including the 2025 report on its website. We do not quote hardness or contaminant numbers from memory. Read the report or test your tap, then use that information to choose a system. We can help you read the results.

Installing a softener or filter in a desert home

A softener needs a drain for its regeneration cycle, an outlet and space near the main line. Garages and utility closets are common spots. We check your water pressure, the line size and where the system will drain, and we add a bypass so the system can be serviced without shutting off water to the house.

Salt-based softeners add sodium to the wastewater, and some communities limit them. Ask us about local rules, and check with your sewer provider. On a septic property, the brine discharge can affect the tank, so we talk through the setup before installing.

Look for treatment products tested to NSF and ANSI standards for the claims they make, and note that connections follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Questions to ask before you buy a system

Ask what the system removes, what it does not remove, what it costs to run each year and who services it. Ask whether the salesperson tested your actual water or quoted a generic number. A seller who will not show you test results is selling a box, not a solution.

Be careful with free water test offers at the door. A real test uses a lab or a calibrated kit and gives numbers you can keep. If the demo changes color in a way that cannot be repeated, skip it and read the district report instead.

Maintenance you should plan for

Every system has a running cost. A softener needs salt and a check of the brine tank now and then. A carbon filter needs a cartridge change on schedule. A reverse osmosis unit needs filters and, less often, a membrane. If you skip maintenance, the system stops working well and may harm what it is meant to protect.

We can set up a schedule and leave you a short care sheet. For rentals, a labeled system and a service date on the unit helps the next person who opens the cabinet.

Water heaters, appliances and the case for treatment

The strongest case for a softener is protecting equipment. Heaters, dishwashers and washers all lose efficiency when scale builds up. If your heater rumbles or your tankless unit throws scale errors, treatment may pay for itself over time. Our tankless water heater page explains flushing, and it works well with a softener.
