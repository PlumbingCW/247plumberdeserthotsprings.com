Bathroom Plumbing Work in Desert Hot Springs, CA: What a Remodel Involves

Bathroom plumbing happens in two stages. The rough-in puts the pipes in the walls and floor before the finishes go on, and the final sets the fixtures after tile and paint. Moving a toilet, shower or sink changes the rough-in, and that is where the cost and the permit questions arise.

The cheapest bathroom remodel keeps the fixtures where they are. Every move costs money because it changes drains and vents.

Rough-in: what goes in before the walls close

  • Water supply lines with shutoff valves for each fixture
  • Drain lines with the right slope to the main
  • Vent pipes so drains flow and traps hold water
  • A shower valve body and pan set in the right position
  • Blocking in the wall for grab bars, towel bars and vanities

Everything in the rough-in must be right before drywall goes up. A drain with too little slope clogs, and a vent that is missing makes fixtures gurgle. An inspector checks the rough-in, and pressure tests are often required before the walls are closed.

A toilet drain is larger than a sink drain, and it needs more slope. That is why moving a toilet is a bigger job. On a raised floor, the drain can be rerouted below. On a concrete slab, the plumber has to cut the slab, trench, lay new pipe and patch the concrete. Our plumbing installation page covers what we do on a slab.

Shower pans, valves and the leaks behind tile

Most remodel leaks are in showers. A pan that is not watertight, a valve that is not sealed or a drain that is not clamped properly can leak into a wall for months. Flood test the pan before tile goes on. Fill it with water, mark the level and wait a day. If the level drops, fix it before you build over it.

Choose a valve that you can service. Some valve bodies can be changed through the trim opening, and others require opening the wall. Ask your plumber what is behind your wall before you choose a style.

Desert Hot Springs: permits and older homes

The City of Desert Hot Springs Building Division issues plumbing permits and inspects the work. Moving or adding fixtures generally involves drain and vent changes, so ask the division at (760) 329-6411 what your project needs.

Homes in the original townsite, centered on Palm Drive and Pierson Boulevard, are among the oldest in the city. Older homes may have galvanized supply pipe or cast iron drains behind the walls. A remodel is the best time to replace them, since the walls are already open.

Fixture clearances, drain slope and venting follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the inspector uses it at the rough-in stage.

Order of work in a typical remodel

1

Demolition and inspection

The old fixtures come out and we check pipes and framing for problems.

2

Rough-in

New supply, drain and vent lines are set and tested.

3

Inspection and closing

The inspector checks the rough-in, then the walls and floor are closed and finished.

4

Final fixtures

Toilet, sink, faucets and shower trim are set and tested.

Plan so the plumber is on site at the rough-in and the final, not once for everything. Missing the rough-in window means opening finished walls later. If you hire a contractor, ask who coordinates the plumber and the inspection.

Water efficiency and everyday comfort

A remodel is the time to choose water efficient fixtures. Low flow toilets and showerheads use less water and reduce your bill. Check the water pressure first, since some low flow fixtures need decent pressure to work well. If hot water is slow to arrive, a short run from the heater or a recirculation line can help. See our toilet repair page for older toilets, and note we serve Palm Springs and the wider service area too.

Budget for what you cannot see

The surprises in a bathroom remodel are behind the walls. Corroded supply lines, a rotted subfloor or a drain that has to be replaced all show up once the demolition starts. Set aside a cushion in the budget, and ask the plumber to look at the pipes as soon as the walls are open.

It also helps to decide on fixtures before the rough-in. The valve for a rain shower, a body spray or a handheld unit depends on the trim you choose. Changing the style after the rough-in can mean opening a finished wall. Pick the fixtures first, then set the plumbing to match.
