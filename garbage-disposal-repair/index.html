Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation in Desert Hot Springs, CA

A disposal that hums, jams or leaks is usually a quick repair or a straightforward swap. We handle both and check the sink drain while we are there.

Never put your hand in a garbage disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs, and switch off power at the wall switch and the breaker first.

Most disposal problems fall into three groups: a jam, an electrical issue or a leak. A jam usually clears with a hex key turned in the bottom of the unit, and the reset button on the underside trips when the motor overloads. If neither works, or if water is leaking from the body, call us.

Repair or replace a disposal

A disposal that is a few years old and has a simple jam is worth repairing. A unit that leaks from the housing is finished, because the casing cannot be sealed. The same is true when the motor has burned out. We compare the cost of both options and install a new unit when it costs less over time.

New installs include mounting the flange, connecting the drain and dishwasher line, wiring or plugging in the unit and testing for leaks. If you are adding a disposal where there was none, we check for an outlet, a switch and whether the sink drain is a good match.

Keeping a disposal and a kitchen drain healthy

  • Run cold water before, during and after use
  • Cut large scraps into small pieces
  • Skip grease, coffee grounds, eggshell piles, pasta and rice in volume
  • Avoid fibrous items like celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves
  • Clean the unit now and then with ice and citrus peel

Many disposal complaints are really drain clogs. If water backs up into the second sink bowl or the dishwasher, the line past the disposal is partly blocked. We check that before we replace anything, so you do not pay for a new unit that fails again.

On a property with a septic system, a disposal adds solids to the tank. Ask us whether a disposal makes sense in your case. If you live in the northern part of town where Mission Springs Water District is moving homes from septic to sewer, your setup may change soon.

Disposal drains connect through a trap that follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the dishwasher line needs an air gap or high loop.

Installing a new disposal, step by step

A swap takes under two hours in most kitchens. We shut off power, disconnect the old unit, clean the sink flange and mount the new one. Then we reconnect the drain and the dishwasher hose, wire or plug in the unit and run water to check every joint.

The dishwasher knockout plug is the detail most DIY installs miss. If the plug is left in, the dishwasher cannot drain, and the water backs up into the dishwasher. We check it on every install, and we test the dishwasher before we leave.

Rentals and busy kitchens

Vacation rentals and shared kitchens put food waste through a disposal that was not designed for it. Guests do not know what to avoid, and the result is a jam every few weeks. If you manage a rental, a heavier duty unit and a short note near the sink about what not to put down can cut the number of calls. Our commercial plumbing page covers multi-unit properties.
