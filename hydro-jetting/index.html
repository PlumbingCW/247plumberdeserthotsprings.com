A snake cuts a hole. A jet washes the pipe. That difference is why a line cabled three times in a year usually needs jetting on the fourth visit.

If your main line is backing up into a tub or floor drain right now, call the 24/7 line and stop using water. Hydro jetting is offered for emergency backups when the line is clear enough to take a jetting hose. If a cable has to open the line first, we do that and then jet.

What hydro jetting does that a cable cannot

A jetting machine pumps water through a hose with a nozzle that fires jets forward and backward. The forward jets break up the blockage, and the rear jets pull the hose through the pipe while scouring the walls. Grease, soap scum and scale come off the full circumference instead of leaving a narrow channel through the middle.

That matters in older cast iron and clay lines, where rough walls catch debris, and in restaurant and rental kitchens where grease rebuilds quickly. It also matters for roots. Jetting trims root hairs back to the pipe wall, but it does not repair the joint the roots entered, so a camera look follows when roots are the cause.

Drain cable

Hydro jetting

  • Pairs well with a camera check
  • Best for repeat or main line clogs

Jetting a main sewer line in Desert Hot Springs

Lines under older streets and slab homes near the original townsite, which was laid out in 1941 around Palm Drive and Pierson Boulevard, can carry decades of buildup. Jetting is a useful first step there because it cleans the pipe before a sewer camera inspection, so the camera sees pipe instead of grease.

Before we jet, we look for a cleanout and ask about the pipe material. Jetting is not right for every line. A pipe that is collapsed, badly offset or held together by old patches can be damaged by pressure, so we start gently and stop if the camera or the response shows a weak section.
