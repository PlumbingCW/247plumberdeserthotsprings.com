Common reasons you have no hot water

Gas unit: pilot or thermocouple

A pilot that will not stay lit or a failed thermocouple stops the burner.

Electric unit: heating element

A burned out element or tripped thermostat leaves you with lukewarm or cold water.

Sediment on the tank bottom

Mineral sediment insulates the burner and makes rumbling or popping sounds.

Failed thermostat or control

The heater runs but the water never reaches the set temperature.

Leaking relief valve

Water at the discharge pipe can mean pressure or temperature trouble.

Corroded tank

Rust stains and leaks at the base mean the tank has failed.

Repair or replace: how we decide

Tank water heaters often last roughly eight to twelve years, and the cost of a repair has to be weighed against the age of the unit. A new thermocouple on a six-year-old heater is an easy call. A leaking tank on a twelve-year-old heater is not. We give you the price of both options so the decision is yours.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters

Gas heaters need a safe flue, combustion air and a correct gas connection. If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Electric heaters need a sound element and thermostat and an electrical circuit in good condition.

Hybrid or heat pump water heaters are more efficient and more complicated, with fans, compressors and condensate lines. We repair the plumbing side and can advise when a service call needs the manufacturer.

Hard desert water and your tank

Mineral buildup is the main reason tank heaters rumble and lose efficiency. Mission Springs Water District publishes an annual water quality report, and the 2025 report is posted on the district website. We do not quote a hardness figure from memory, so test your tap or read the report to see your own numbers, and ask us about flushing the tank and checking the anode rod.

Earthquake bracing also matters here. Bracing a tank heater is standard practice in earthquake country, and the city sits on the Mission Creek Fault. If your heater is not strapped, ask us to add straps during the visit. A tankless unit is another option, covered under tankless water heaters.

Relief valves, discharge lines and venting on a repair follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Pilot lights, vents and gas safety

A gas water heater needs fresh air, a clear vent and a flame that burns blue. A yellow or flickering flame, soot around the vent or a pilot that keeps going out are signs to stop and call. Carbon monoxide has no smell, so a working alarm near the heater and sleeping areas is a basic safety step.

If you smell gas, do not touch switches or try to relight anything. Leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
