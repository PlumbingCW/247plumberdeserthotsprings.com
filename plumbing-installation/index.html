Planning the plumbing for a remodel

A drain needs slope to work, and a vent needs a path to the roof. That is why moving a toilet a few feet can be easy in one house and difficult in another. On a slab, a new drain means cutting concrete. In a crawl space or on a raised floor, the same move is simpler.

Before demolition starts, we walk the space, check what is in the walls and flag anything that changes the plan. Older homes may have galvanized supply or cast iron drains that should be replaced while the walls are open, and we tell you so up front. If the supply pipe is aging, repiping during the remodel can be cheaper than opening the wall twice.

Permits, inspections and manufactured homes

The City of Desert Hot Springs Building Division issues plumbing permits and inspects the work. Plumbing work beyond a simple fixture swap generally needs a permit, and we handle the application with you. For permit specifics, the division lists a phone of (760) 329-6411.

Manufactured and mobile homes have different pipe and drain layouts and different rules, and some parks have their own requirements. Tell us what you have so we bring the right parts. If a fixture is only dripping, faucet repair may be all you need, and toilet repair covers a worn toilet.

Fixture spacing, venting and drain slope follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the permit inspection checks the rough-in.

Before the crew arrives

Clear the work area, move cabinet contents and protect nearby floors. Have the fixtures on site, with the installation sheets, and decide on the final locations. Changes after demolition start cost time and money, so settle the layout first and confirm it with us.

If a general contractor is running the project, give us their contact details. We coordinate the rough-in and the final setting so the inspector can pass each stage on the first visit.

Shower and tub valves, pans and leaks behind the wall

Showers are the most common place for a hidden leak after a remodel. The valve, the pan and the drain have to work together, and a mistake leaks into the wall for months before anyone sees it. We pressure test new supply lines and flood test a shower pan before it is tiled, so a leak shows up while the wall is still open.

If you are replacing an old tub or shower, tell us the brand of the existing valve. Some valves can take a new trim kit, which is far cheaper than opening the wall to swap the valve body.

Gas lines, outdoor kitchens and spa plumbing

Desert homes use outdoor space year round, and many have outdoor kitchens, barbecue gas lines, misting systems or spa and pool equipment. Each of these needs a correctly sized line, a shutoff and a pressure test. We handle the plumbing side and tell you when a licensed specialist for another trade is needed.
